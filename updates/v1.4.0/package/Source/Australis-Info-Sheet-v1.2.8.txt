AUSTRALIS-CLASS CVN
Royal Australian Navy - fictional alternative-history carrier

ROLE
Naval strike, fleet air defence and long-range sea control across the Pacific and Indian Oceans. The Australis class represents an alternative Australian carrier programme developed after the Second World War and Korean War, with an emphasis on sustained operations, crew protection and enclosed aviation facilities.

SHIP PARTICULARS
Length: 370 m
Configured displacement: approximately 112,000 tonnes
Maximum speed: 30 knots
Propulsion: nuclear power, 280,000 hp and eight waterjet propulsion units
Scenario service window: 2025-2055

AIR OPERATIONS
Capacity: up to 99 aircraft in total
Four 95 m catapults: two forward and two on the port waist
Four aircraft lifts, an angled arrested-recovery lane and three helicopter/VTOL pads
Enclosed hangar and lift arrangements for operations in exposed ocean conditions
Expanded stock-aircraft catalogue covering fixed-wing, STOL, VTOL and helicopters; actual deck compatibility varies by airframe

DEFENCE AND SENSORS
Six CIWS stations: five fixed and one forward retractable station
Two 30 mm gun mounts, layered surface-to-air missile launchers and four VLS groups
Chaff countermeasures and two towed decoys
Air and surface search, aviation-control radar, fire-control directors, electronic support and defensive/offensive electronic countermeasures

DECK SYSTEMS
Controller-managed weapon bays, forward CIWS6 and ICCS deck-control bubble
Landing guidance, navigation lights and deck, hangar, lift-well and tower lighting for night and low-light operations

CLASS MEMBERS
HMAS Australis 91
HMAS Southern Cross 92
HMAS Federation 93
HMAS Commonwealth 94

OPERATIONAL NOTES
Requires AnchorChain and its current official preloader. Existing air wings are preserved; 99 is capacity, not an automatic embarked total. Missile and sensor performance follows the configured Sea Power definitions. Mechanisms depend on controller startup and their native threat/flight-traffic conditions.
